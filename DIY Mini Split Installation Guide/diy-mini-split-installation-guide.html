DIY vs. Professional Installation

You can handle some preparation yourself or hire an installer to manage the job.

Do the prep yourself

Check the equipment and prepare the space. Before doing any work, confirm that your installer accepts the preparation you plan to do.

  • Agree on your tasks and the installer’s responsibilities
  • Arrange electrical and refrigerant work before you start
Review prep checklist

Hire an installer

Provide your room and equipment details, then ask for a quote that spells out the installation work included.

  • Check whether electrical work and permits are included
  • Confirm who handles testing and startup

Planning multiple rooms or a ceiling-mounted system?

More indoor units mean more routes and drains to coordinate. Ceiling-mounted and concealed units also need suitable space and access. Review the layout with an installer before choosing equipment.

Before you order: four things to check

Confirm these details before buying equipment or booking the installation.

  • System & parts

    Check your model’s manual and packing list before ordering extras.

  • Electrical supply

    Have an electrician confirm voltage, panel capacity and circuit requirements.

  • Placement & piping

    Review unit locations, pipe length, drainage and clearances with your installer.

  • Permits & installation

    Confirm local approvals and who is qualified to perform the work.

More about these checks
Check your model and included parts
Confirm the system size and indoor and outdoor model numbers. Use the matching manual and packing list to check what comes with the system and what you need to buy separately.
Have an electrician check the circuit
Check the model’s voltage and circuit requirements. Ask an electrician to confirm panel capacity and any circuit or breaker work needed before installation.
Confirm unit locations and pipe routes
Review the unit positions, pipe sizes and length, drain route, and wall opening with your installer before drilling. Allow the clearances in the manual for airflow and maintenance.
Arrange permits and installation help
Check local permits and any HOA or landlord approval. Agree who handles electrical work, refrigerant connections and final checks. Refrigerant work can require EPA certification; electrical work may require a licensed professional. EPA certification guidance

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a vacuum pump for a mini split?

Check the installation instructions for your model. Vacuum-pump requirements depend on the system and installation method. Use a qualified technician for work that requires certification or specialist skills.

Does DIY installation affect warranty or support?

Check the warranty terms and installation instructions for your model before starting. Ask DELLA support about any requirements you are unsure of; do not assume DIY work is covered.
