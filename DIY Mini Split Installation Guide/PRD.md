## 1. Objective

Create a Della topical landing page titled:

**DIY Mini Split Installation Guide**

The page should capture research-oriented organic traffic and serve Google/Facebook ad visitors who are considering a Della mini split but need installation confidence before purchase.

Primary decision:

> Can I plan a DIY mini split install, and what should I buy or confirm before checkout?

The page should help visitors understand what they can plan, what parts/tools may be needed, and when electrical, refrigerant, code, or commissioning work may require a qualified professional. After that decision framework is clear, it should route visitors to Della mini split systems, wall-mounted mini splits, accessories, the rental install kit, the line set product, installation videos, manuals/support, and partner installer resources.

## 2. Page Type And Positioning

Page type:

**Installer-support research landing page + light collection-support page.**

This is not:

- a pure article
- a hard-sell mini split collection page
- a BTU calculator
- a technical installation manual
- a coupon/sale page
- a guarantee that every visitor can fully DIY install

Positioning statement:

> Before you order, know what you can handle, what you need to check, and what Della paths support the project.

Recommended balance:

- First 40%: installation readiness, DIY-vs-pro boundaries, path selection
- Middle 35%: high-level install steps, tools, parts, accessories, before-checkout checks
- Final 25%: installation essentials cards, system path guidance, videos/manuals/support, FAQ, bottom CTA

The page should be content-led and decision-led, but still DTC-commerce aware. It should not feel like a generic blog or an aggressive product wall.

## 4. Audience Interpretation And Brand Risk

From a Della brand and DTC landing-page perspective, the phrase `DIY Mini Split Installation Guide` pulls in several intent groups at once:

- **Hands-on DIY planners** who want to know whether they can do the job themselves.
- **Risk-aware homeowners** who are interested in saving money but nervous about electrical, refrigerant, permits, warranty, or making a costly mistake.
- **Parts/tool shoppers** who already expect to buy a system and need the right line set, accessories, or install tools.
- **Ad visitors** who may not be deeply technical and need a confident, official path before they click into products.
- **Comparison shoppers** who may have seen DIY-focused competitors and need to know whether Della can support a similar project without overpromising.

The page should therefore feel like an official installation preparation hub, not a hobbyist tutorial. If the page looks too technical, Della may inherit liability and warranty confusion. If it looks too commercial, research users will distrust it. If it looks like a generic blog, ad traffic will not see a clear next step.

Brand stance:

- Be practical, calm, and support-led.
- Let users feel Della understands DIY planning without pretending every install is fully DIY.
- Make the next step obvious: system, accessories, tools, installer, videos, manuals, or support.
- Use product and support paths as part of the decision journey, not as a sales interruption.

## 6. Safety, Legal, And Warranty Guardrails

The page must not encourage unsafe or non-compliant work.

Use conservative wording around:

- refrigerant
- line-set connections and evacuation
- vacuum/leak check
- electrical circuit, breaker, disconnect, and wiring
- permits, HOA, landlord rules, and local code
- warranty impact
- final commissioning

External source guidance:

- EPA Section 608 Q&A states that adding/removing refrigerant from a mini split as part of installation, and connecting or disconnecting hoses or pre-charged lines, requires Section 608 technician certification.
- EPA Section 608 technician certification requirements include attaching/detaching hoses and gauges and adding/removing refrigerant.
- DOE/Energy.gov notes ductless mini splits can be simpler to install than whole-house systems, but correct sizing and indoor unit location are crucial and users should consult a professional for proper sizing and installation.

Allowed wording:

- `Use this guide to plan the project, understand the steps, and prepare parts/tools.`
- `Some homeowners handle planning and mounting preparation, while electrical or refrigerant-related work may require a licensed or certified professional depending on local rules and the exact system.`
- `Confirm requirements for your model, home, and local jurisdiction before starting.`

Disallowed wording:

- `Anyone can install it without a pro.`
- `No permit needed.`
- `DIY installation never affects warranty.`
- `No HVAC technician required.`
- Detailed refrigerant charging, bypass, or electrical wiring instructions.

Warranty:

- Do not claim DIY does or does not affect warranty unless Della's current warranty language is separately verified and approved.
- FAQ can say to check the exact model manual and Della support/warranty terms.
