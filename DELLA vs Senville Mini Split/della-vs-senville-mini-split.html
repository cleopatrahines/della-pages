What Forum Buyers Debate Most

  • When winter heating is the primary load, compare HSPF2 and low-ambient ratings rather than cooling SEER2. On 230V everyday models, DELLA Vario (9.5 HSPF2, −4°F) beats Senville LETO (9.1 HSPF2, 5°F). For sub-zero climates, DELLA Optima Pro ties Senville AURA at 10.5 HSPF2 (Region IV), AHRI/ENERGY STAR certification, and −22°F rated operation.

  • Both DELLA and Senville ship with pre-flared 16 ft copper line sets and pre-charged condensers—but neither uses no-vacuum quick-connect fittings. You still need a torque wrench, vacuum pump, and micron gauge to pull below 500 microns. Senville voids warranty without a licensed HVAC invoice. DELLA keeps registered DIY installs under lifetime parts coverage and rents the full evacuation & torque kit (7- or 15-day plan with refundable deposit and prepaid return); if you use the kit, add your install video link when you register.

  • Buying 2 to 4 units for a detached garage, barn, or woodworking shop? Separate 18K or 24K single-zone systems usually deliver higher efficiency, shorter copper runs, lower upfront cost, and backup heating if one unit needs service mid-winter. Choose Multi-Zone only when exterior pad space or HOA rules restrict you to one condenser.

  • Forum threads often guess Senville is built by Midea and count on that for third-party parts; Senville itself does not name its manufacturer. Senville does publish lower minimum fan noise (LETO 22.5 dB on 230V or 17.5 dB on 110V, AURA 24 dB, vs. 27–30 dBA on DELLA 12K models; the Optima Pro CloudAir 9K lists 26 dB). DELLA counters with direct 24/7 US support, 30-day returns, CloudAir soft indirect airflow on Optima and Optima Pro, and Limited Lifetime Parts Coverage when registered within 100 days.

Frequently Asked Questions

Comparing two models? Ask a DELLA specialist
Are DELLA mini splits any good compared to Senville?

Yes—provided you compare matching tiers. DELLA Vario 12K (23 SEER2, 9.5 HSPF2, −4°F) out-specs Senville LETO 230V (21.3 SEER2, 9.1 HSPF2, 5°F), and DELLA Optima Pro CloudAir ties Senville AURA at 10.5 HSPF2 and −22°F with AHRI & ENERGY STAR listings. Senville holds an edge on published minimum indoor noise and a longer US forum track record; DELLA wins on DIY warranty eligibility and Limited Lifetime Parts Coverage.

Does DIY installation void the Senville or DELLA warranty?

Senville states that installation, pressure testing, and evacuation must be performed by a licensed HVAC technician for its warranty to remain valid—self-installation voids coverage. DELLA’s lifetime parts coverage, compressor included, applies to both DIY and licensed pro installs registered within 100 days. If you install with the DELLA Rental Install Kit (7- or 15-day rental with refundable deposit and prepaid return label), add your install video link when you register. Labor is excluded on both brands, and after the first year you pay part shipping.

DELLA Optima Pro vs. Senville AURA: which is better for sub-zero winters?

Both are rated down to −22°F (−30°C) with 10.5 HSPF2 (Region IV) and R-454B refrigerant. Senville AURA publishes slightly lower minimum indoor noise (24 dB vs. 30 dB) and 25.4 vs. 25 SEER2. DELLA Optima Pro adds CloudAir indirect louver airflow and Limited Lifetime Parts Coverage that also covers registered DIY installs. If your winters rarely drop below −10°F, DELLA Optima (−13°F) is a lighter-duty cold-climate option.

Do DELLA and Senville come with line sets, or do I need extra parts?

Both include a standard 16 ft (16.4 ft on DELLA Vario and Optima Pro) pre-flared copper line set and communication wire in the box, with pre-charged outdoor condensers. Neither is a no-vacuum quick-connect system—you still need a vacuum pump, micron gauge, valve core tools, and torque wrench (available through DELLA’s Rental Install Kit), plus a wall bracket or ground pad, electrical whip, and outdoor disconnect.

For a garage or workshop needing 2–4 units, should I buy multi-zone or separate single-zone units?

Separate 18K or 24K single-zone systems (such as DELLA Vario 18K or 24K) are usually the better move for workshops and detached buildings: they keep copper runs short, hit higher real-world efficiency, and leave you with working heat if one unit ever needs service. Pick a Multi-Zone bundle when exterior wall space or HOA rules allow only one outdoor condenser.

Should I choose 115V or 230V for a 12,000 BTU mini split?

Choose 208/230V (DELLA Vario 12K or Optima Pro 12K) whenever your panel can support a dedicated 230V breaker—you gain higher SEER2/HSPF2 efficiency and much stronger winter heating (−4°F to −22°F). Choose 115V (DELLA Motto 12K) when adding a 230V circuit is impractical in a bedroom, shed, or mild-winter room.
