Quick answer

Which type fits your space?

A ceiling cassette sits in the ceiling and sends air in multiple directions, which works well for open rooms, finished living areas, or layouts where you want to keep walls clear. A wall mount installs high on a wall, sends air across the room, and usually offers easier access with a lower install path.

  • Open floor plan or large room: start with Ceiling Cassette.
  • Bedroom, office, garage, or rental: start with Wall Mount.
  • Not sure: use the decision guide and table below.

Head-to-head comparison

What changes between ceiling cassette and wall mount?

Both are ductless mini split indoor unit styles. The better choice usually comes down to placement, ceiling access, airflow needs, and how visible you want the indoor unit to be.

Installation and value

Installation cost depends on more than the indoor unit price

Wall mount is usually the simpler install path because it needs wall placement, a bracket, and a planned line-set route. Ceiling cassette often adds ceiling opening, framing clearance, service access, and condensate drainage planning. Treat product price and installation complexity as one decision.
