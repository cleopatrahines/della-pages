Plan a Manufactured-Home Installation

Most mobile-home install headaches come down to mounting, drainage, and electrical prep—confirm these four points before ordering.

  1. Set the outdoor unit on a level concrete pad or ground stand beside your skirting to prevent wall vibration and noise. Browse mounting stands and line-set accessories.

  2. Manufactured walls can flex slightly over time. Fasten mounting plates into solid studs (or a stud-spanning backer board) and keep gravity drain lines pitched continuously outward.

  3. Single-zone 115V units and 230V multi-zone systems still need dedicated breaker capacity confirmed by an electrician. Confirm insulation and sun exposure in our mobile home sizing guide or sizing calculator.

  4. Ask local pros upfront if they service manufactured homes—or if you have qualified HVAC installation experience, review model specs and check our DIY Install Kit Rental.

Why do some HVAC contractors hesitate to install mini splits on mobile homes?

Some technicians avoid manufactured homes because thin wall paneling, lighter framing, and seasonal settling can cause indoor drain pans to tilt or wall-hung condensers to vibrate. Mounting indoor brackets directly into wall studs (or a stud-spanning backer board) and setting the outdoor unit on a level ground pad addresses these concerns.

Should I remove or seal my old underbelly ducts?

You can keep an existing furnace in good condition as extreme-cold backup, or seal off leaky floor registers and abandoned underbelly ducts so conditioned air does not escape beneath the home. Ask your installer whether disconnecting old vents makes sense for your setup.

Can a mini split replace electric baseboard, furnace, or propane heat?

Yes, every DELLA system featured on this page is a heat-pump mini split that provides both heating and cooling. Cold-weather output varies by series, so compare rated low-temperature ranges and consider Optima Ultra Heat models if winter heating is a primary goal.

Can a 100-amp mobile home electrical panel handle a mini split?

Many mobile homes with 100-amp service can add a single-zone unit or a single 230V multi-zone system, depending on existing appliances such as electric water heaters, ranges, and dryers. Have a licensed electrician verify available panel space and each model’s circuit requirements before ordering.

Why are only wall-mounted mini splits listed here instead of ceiling cassettes?

Most manufactured and mobile homes have shallow roof trusses with limited ceiling cavity depth, making recessed ceiling cassettes and concealed ducted air handlers difficult or impractical to fit. High-wall indoor heads mount inside the living space and connect through a small exterior wall opening.
