Not sure? Check your room conditions

Look beyond square footage

Room size is only part of the picture. These details help explain what your space needs.

Sun exposure changes the load

Large windows and direct afternoon sun can increase cooling demand, even when the floor area stays the same.

Count the space that connects

Measure the whole area you plan to condition. An adjoining room without a closing door adds space beyond the room you first measured.

Check what keeps heat in or out

Note insulation in walls and ceilings, along with drafts around doors and windows. Better insulation can reduce heating and cooling demand.

Look above the floor plan

Measure ceiling height, including vaulted areas. Extra air volume can change the load even when the floor area is unchanged.

Consider how the room is used

List regular occupants and heat-producing appliances. Kitchens and workshops can add loads that floor area alone does not describe.

Review these conditions together with your installer, including your local climate.

Bigger is not always better

An oversized system may cycle off too soon, leaving less time to remove moisture while cooling. Match capacity to the room for steadier comfort.

Before you choose 12K or 18K

Is 12,000 BTU or 18,000 BTU better for my room?

Start with the room itself, not the bigger number. Choose 12K for a smaller enclosed room; check 18K when the space is open, sunny, high-ceiling, or harder to cool.

How many square feet can a 12,000 BTU mini split cover?

A 12,000 BTU mini split is commonly used as a reference for enclosed rooms up to 550 sq. ft. If the room opens into another area or gets strong sun, do not size by square footage alone.

How many square feet can an 18,000 BTU mini split cover?

An 18,000 BTU mini split is commonly used as a reference for larger or more open rooms up to 1,000 sq. ft. It is a stronger starting point, but the room layout and heat load still matter.

Is bigger always safer when choosing between 12K and 18K?

No. If the room is smaller and enclosed, 18K may be more capacity than the space needs; if it is open, sunny, or high-load, 18K is worth checking.

Should I choose 18K for a garage, sunroom, or open living area?

Usually, start by checking 18K for those spaces. Garages, sunrooms, and open layouts often need more capacity because of sun, insulation, air volume, and stored heat.
