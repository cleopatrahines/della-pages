Area fits 12K, but the room is sunny or open? Check room conditions

Look beyond square footage

Check the layout, windows, insulation, ceiling height, and heat sources in your room.

Check direct sun

Large windows and direct afternoon sun can increase cooling demand. Note which windows receive sun and for how long.

Include adjoining rooms

Measure the full area you want to cool or heat, including adjoining rooms that cannot be closed off.

Check insulation and drafts

Check wall and ceiling insulation and note drafts around doors or windows. Better insulation can reduce the heating and cooling load.

Measure ceiling height

Measure the ceiling height, including vaulted areas. A higher ceiling adds air volume and can change the capacity needed.

Count people and heat sources

Note how many people regularly use the room and which appliances give off heat. Include heat sources in adjoining kitchens or workshops.

Bigger is not always better

An oversized system may cycle off before it has removed enough moisture, leaving the room cool but humid. Choose capacity for the room's load rather than sizing up as a precaution.

Before you choose 12K or 18K

Is 12,000 BTU or 18,000 BTU better for my room?

12K is a starting point for smaller, enclosed rooms. Check 18K for larger or connected spaces, strong sun, or high ceilings. If your room fits parts of both descriptions, ask an installer to assess the load and local climate before choosing.

How many square feet can a 12,000 BTU mini split cover?

The 12K models shown here list coverage up to 550 sq. ft. as a reference. Strong sun, high ceilings, poor insulation, or adjoining open rooms can change the capacity needed.

How many square feet can an 18,000 BTU mini split cover?

The 18K models shown here list coverage up to 1,000 sq. ft. as a reference. This is not a guaranteed fit for every room of that size; layout, insulation, sun, and ceiling height affect the load.

Is bigger always safer when choosing between 12K and 18K?

No. A system that is too large may run in short cycles and remove less moisture while cooling. A smaller, enclosed room may be better served by 12K than 18K.

Should I choose 18K for a garage, sunroom, or open living area?

18K is worth checking, but it may not be the right size for every garage or sunroom. These spaces can have strong sun, limited insulation, or extra heat sources. Include those conditions in the sizing assessment.
