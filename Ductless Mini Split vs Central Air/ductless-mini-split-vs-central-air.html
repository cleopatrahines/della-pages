Mini Split vs Central Air: What Actually Changes?

Both paths can provide home comfort. The main differences are how air is distributed, how rooms are controlled, and what the installation requires.

CompareWall-Mounted Mini SplitCentral Air
DistributionIndoor units serve individual rooms or zones.An air handler distributes air through ducts and registers.
Whole-Home DuctworkNot required.Core part of the distribution system.
Room ControlStrong room-by-room or zone flexibility.Primarily centralized whole-home distribution.
Indoor EquipmentWall-mounted indoor units remain visible.Rooms mainly use supply and return registers.
Installation ScopeIndoor units, line sets, drains, electrical, and outdoor unit.Air handler, condenser, ductwork, line set, and electrical.
Retrofit FlexibilityCan address selected spaces without rebuilding whole-home ducts.Usually planned around a whole-home ducted system.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between a ductless mini split and central air?

A wall-mounted ductless mini split delivers heating and cooling directly through indoor units in individual rooms or zones. Central air uses an air handler and duct system to distribute conditioned air throughout the home.

Can a ductless mini split heat and cool an entire house?

It can be used for multi-room and whole-home ductless projects when the number of zones, equipment capacity, room loads, and installation plan are properly matched. Home layout and professional sizing still matter.

Can I add a mini split if I already have central air?

Yes. A separate mini split can be considered for a garage, addition, converted space, or problem room while the existing central system continues serving the rest of the home.

Does central air require ductwork?

Central air distributes conditioned air through ducts and room registers. If the home does not have usable ductwork, adding or replacing the duct system becomes part of the project.

Is a mini split always more efficient than central air?

Not necessarily. Actual energy use depends on equipment efficiency, system sizing, duct condition, zoning, climate, installation quality, and how the home is used.

How should I size a mini split or central air system?

Manufacturer coverage ranges are useful shopping references, but final sizing should account for the heating and cooling load, insulation, windows, ceiling height, climate, duct conditions, and installation requirements.
