Right-size check

Bigger Is Not Always Better

A right-sized system should run steadily enough to manage comfort and humidity. Use this check before comparing product models.

Too Small

May run constantly and struggle on high-load days.

  • Longer run times
  • Higher energy use
  • Less comfort on hot days

Right Size

Supports steadier comfort and better humidity control.

  • More consistent comfort
  • Better humidity control
  • More balanced operation

Too Large

May short cycle, cool unevenly, and reduce humidity control.

  • Short cycling
  • Uneven comfort
  • Higher upfront cost

Mini Split Size Guide FAQs

What size mini split do I need?

Start with the room size range, then adjust for insulation, sun exposure, ceiling height, layout, and room use. For many Della shoppers, the first decision is choosing between 9K, 12K, 18K, 24K, and 36K BTU collection paths.

Is 12,000 BTU enough for a bedroom or studio?

12K is a common starting point for rooms around 401-550 sq ft. If the room has poor insulation, large windows, or high sun exposure, review the adjustment factors before choosing.

When should I move from 12K to 18K?

Consider moving up when the space is larger than the 12K range, has an open connection to nearby rooms, or carries extra heat load from sun, equipment, or frequent activity. An installer can confirm edge cases.

Is a 24K mini split too large for one room?

It can be too large for smaller enclosed rooms. 24K is better treated as a large open-space starting point, not a universal upgrade.

Can one 36K mini split cool multiple rooms?

A large-capacity unit may help a very open layout, but separated rooms usually need a different system plan. For independent comfort in multiple rooms, compare single-zone and multi-zone options before buying.

Why should I not oversize a mini split?

An oversized system may short cycle, which can create uneven comfort and weaker humidity control. The goal is a right-sized system, not simply the largest BTU available.
